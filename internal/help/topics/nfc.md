# NFC tags

An NFC tag stuck to an asset can hold its scan link, the same link its QR code holds. Holding an unlocked phone against the tag opens the asset's scan page, with no app and nothing to line up: an iPhone XS or later reads tags by itself, and so does an Android phone with NFC turned on. A tag works in the dark, inside a cabinet, and under a worn or dirty label.

## Writing a tag

**Write tag**, on an asset's page and its scan page, writes its scan link to a tag. In Chrome on Android, tap it and hold a blank tag against the back of the phone until it says the tag is written. It then offers to lock the tag, so that no one can write over it. Locking can't be undone: a locked tag can never be written again, not even by BAM!.

Other browsers, including every browser on an iPhone, can't write tags. There, **Write tag** shows the link with **Copy**: paste it into an app that writes tags, such as NFC Tools, as a link (URL) record.

A tag holds the server's address, as a QR code does. If BAM! moves to a new address, write its tags again, as its QR codes must be printed again.

## Which tags to buy

- **NTAG213, NTAG215 or NTAG216** stickers, which every phone that reads NFC reads. An NTAG213, the smallest and cheapest, holds a scan link easily.
- **On-metal tags** for anything metal: an ordinary tag stuck straight onto metal can't be read.
- Tags read from 2 to 4 cm away, so put them where a phone can reach, and mark where they are.

## What a tag protects

A tag holds only a link, and anyone's phone can read it, copy it, or write another tag just like it. Signing in is what protects what BAM! holds: the scan page shows only what the person holding the phone may see, as it does for a QR code.
