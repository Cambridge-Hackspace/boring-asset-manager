# Labels and printing

**Templates** lay out labels: text, codes such as QR codes, and images, at the size of your label stock. Text uses placeholders filled in for each asset, such as `{{asset.name}}`, `{{asset.location}}` or `{{field.serial}}`; the editor suggests them as you type.

`{{scan_url}}` is the address of an asset's scan page: a QR code of it takes whoever scans the label there. See [Scanning](/help/scanning).

The editor is laid out like a drawing program. The **toolbox** on the left holds the tools: **Select and move**, then **Text**, **Barcode or QR code**, **Image** and **Icon**, then **Rectangle**, **Ellipse**, **Line** and **Polygon**, and **Import SVG**. Choose one and drag on the label to draw what it adds, or click to place one at its usual size; chosen from the keyboard, it is added in the middle of the label. Across the top, the tool's options give the selection's position, size and angle in numbers. The panels on the right, **Properties**, **Layers**, **Arrange**, **Label**, **Printer** and **Preview data**, each fold away by their title and move up or down, and the editor remembers how you left them. The status bar along the bottom shows where the pointer is on the label and what you can do now.

Click an element to select it, and Shift+click to add or remove others; where elements overlap, Ctrl+click takes the one beneath. Drag on an empty part of the label to select with a box: dragged to the right, it takes what it covers wholly; to the left, what it touches. Drag an element to move it, holding Shift to keep to one direction. Drag a handle to resize, holding Shift to keep its proportions or Ctrl to resize from the middle. Grab just outside a corner to turn it: it snaps to multiples of 45° as it passes them, unless **Snap turns to 45°** is switched off, turns in 15° steps with Ctrl, and freely with Shift. Elements keep to the grid, except while Shift is held, and Escape puts back a drag in progress. While you drag, its size, angle or place shows beside the pointer. Right-click for a menu of what can be done to the selection.

Dragged elements snap to what is near: the grid, guides, the edges and middles of other elements, and the label's edges and middle, with a line showing what they met. Choose what they snap to under **Snap to**, at the top right; hold Shift to place them freely. Drag from a ruler onto the label for a guide, and drag a guide off the label to take it away; guides are saved with the template, and never print. The **Grid and guides** panel shows the grid, sets how far apart its lines are, where they start, and how finely each step is divided, and shows, locks or deletes the guides.

Across the top, the selection's numbers are given at the point chosen on the small square of nine; Ctrl+click on a handle chooses it too. Lock the proportions to change the width and height together. Lengths can be typed with their unit, such as 5mm or 0.2in. The arrow keys move the selection one grid step, ten with Shift, or a tenth with Alt. Ctrl+A selects everything, Ctrl+Shift+A or Escape nothing. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy and paste elements, between templates too, and pasting SVG imports it. Press Tab, with nothing focused, to hide everything but the label, and Tab again to bring it back.

To look closer, hold Ctrl and turn the mouse wheel, which zooms toward the pointer, or press + and -; the wheel alone scrolls. Hold Space, or the middle button, and drag to move around. 1 shows the label at 100%, 2 fits it, 3 fits its width, and 0 goes between fitting it and where you were. The zoom box at the bottom right takes a percentage. At 100%, each printer dot is one pixel of your screen; switch on **Print size** to see the label at its real size instead, and calibrate your screen once with a ruler, as browsers do not know how large screens are. The rulers mark where the pointer and the selection are; right-click one for millimeters or inches. The **Overview** panel shows the whole label: drag in it to move there.

Elements are drawn in order, each over those before it, so text can sit on an image. The **Layers** panel shows them with the top one first, each with a small picture of what it draws: drag a layer, or use the stacking buttons under **Arrange**, to change what is drawn over what. Where elements overlap, a click selects the top one. The eye beside a layer hides it while you work on what is beneath; it still prints. **Lock** an element so it is not picked up by accident. Double-click a layer, or press F2 on it, to name it. **Arrange** also lines the selection up, with each other or with the label, and spreads three or more out with equal gaps or with their centers evenly apart; several selected can be grouped, so a click takes them all, and a group shows in the layers as a folder you can open, close, hide, lock and name. **History** lists every change you have made: click one to go back to just after it, or on to one you went back from.

Any element can be turned, to any angle: type it in the tool options, or turn it a quarter turn left or right beside it. Several selected elements turn together, about the middle of them all. Codes turned a quarter turn print as sharply as upright ones; at other angles their edges follow the printer's dots, so check that a turned code scans with the scanners you use.

A template is **landscape** when wider than tall and **portrait** when taller. Switching it, or **Turn left** and **Turn right**, turns the label a quarter turn with everything on it, so the design reads the same, turned. **Use its label size** takes a printer's roll with its longer side across, and **Use it upright** the other way round. When a label fits the printer either way, as a square label or one from a continuous roll does, **Feed** chooses which way it goes through: as it fits, or with its width across or along the roll; the fit check says which. A template that fits several label sizes can also take labels turned a quarter turn, its content turned to match.

To add text, choose **Text** and click on the label for a single line, or drag out a box for text that wraps, then type. Double-click a text to type in it again. While you type, the bar across the top sets the font, size, bold and alignment, puts in a field such as the asset's name, and **Fill the box** makes the text as large as its box allows (shrinking it if it must), up to a size you choose. Press Escape, or click elsewhere, when you are done; a text left empty is taken away.

**Shapes** draw borders, dividers, boxes, arrows and backgrounds. Choose **Rectangle**, **Ellipse** or **Line** and drag on the label: hold Shift for a square, a circle or a line at a multiple of 15°, Ctrl to draw from the middle out, and Alt to move it as you draw. A **Polygon** takes a point at each click, and is finished by a double click, Enter, or a click on its first point. The **Pen** draws curves: click for a corner, or drag to pull out a smooth curve (Alt pulls one side only); right-click takes back the last point, a double click or Enter finishes, and a click on the first point closes the shape. Double-click a selected polygon or curve to edit its points: drag them or their handles, double-click the outline to add one, Shift+click one to make it smooth or sharp, press Delete to remove it, and Escape when you are done. **Measure** shows the distance between two points you drag between. Each shape has a stroke and a fill, in black, white or none: white paints over whatever is beneath it, so white text, or a white shape, shows on a black one. Text's **Color** can be white for that too. A rectangle's corners can be rounded. A stroke thinner than one printer dot prints one dot wide, and the editor says so.

**Icon** puts an icon on the label (see **Icons**): each asset's own icon, or its category's, so one template shows a switch on a switch's label and a crate on a crate's; or, under **Properties**, **One icon**, chosen from every icon there is. It prints **Shaded**, its faces in grays, or **As an outline**, which reads better on a small label, and sits in its box to the left, center or right. An asset with no icon leaves its box blank; in the preview with no asset chosen, a question mark stands in.

**Import SVG** brings in a logo, symbol or icon drawn elsewhere, or drop the SVG file on the label: its shapes come in black or white, fitted into the middle of the label and selected together, and print sharp at any size. Colors become black or white by how light they look. Text is left out, as it needs the artwork's fonts, so convert it to paths before exporting the file; images, scripts and effects are left out too, and the editor says what was.

A code keeps **padding** clear around it, inside its box, which scanners use to find where it starts. Resize the code with its handles; its padding is shaded on the label. Set the padding for all sides at once, or press **Each side** to set them one by one, in millimeters or inches, or with the unit typed. Left empty, a side has the code's standard quiet zone, which the field shows, and **Standard** puts every side back to it. A code is made of whole printer dots, so it does not always fill its box exactly: what is left over is shaded too, and said in words. **Across** and **Down** choose where the code sits in its box: centered, or against a side or corner. A margin narrower than the standard is allowed, since a code against the label's edge often scans, but the editor says so: try it with your scanners.

## Printing

On an asset's page, or for several assets ticked on the list, choose a template to download labels as SVG, PNG or PDF, or send them to a printer set up on the **Printers** page. **Images** holds logos and pictures for templates.

Its category's templates are listed first, and the first of them that can label it and that a printer takes is chosen. Only the printers a template fits can be chosen; the others are listed with why not, such as a roll of the wrong size loaded. A template that cannot label an asset, such as one printing a QR code for an asset that has none, says so in place of its preview. On an asset's page, **Add to the queue** keeps its label in the print queue, to print later with others.

Each printer has a roll loaded. Brother QL printers take Brother's own rolls, all of which are listed; TSPL printers such as the Rollo, and DYMO LabelWriters, also take rolls of any size, added under **Label stock** on the **Printers** page with their width, and for die-cut labels their length and the gap between them.

A template has **one label size**, printing only at its own size and DPI, or **fits several label sizes**: designed at one size, it is laid out again for each label it prints on, and at each printer's resolution, so one design serves 29 mm and 62 mm tape, or a Brother and a Rollo. Choose under **Sizing** in the editor, with the narrowest, widest, shortest and tallest labels it takes. Each element then says how it follows the label across and down: in proportion, kept to one side (left or right, top or bottom), stretched between both, or kept in the middle. It can also have a smallest and largest size, and say what happens where it does not fit: the label is refused, it is left off, or something else takes its place, such as the code as text. The editor shows the template on each printer as loaded, and at any size you type, with the sizes it fits in its shape given the longest codes assigned now.

The template editor draws the label as it will print, dot for dot, as you drag, turn and type. Opening the editor loads its renderer first, which takes a moment; until then, or if your browser cannot run it, the label is drawn by the server shortly after each change instead.

A template prints on a printer when its size and DPI suit the roll loaded. The template editor checks against a printer as you design: the one you last chose for the template, or else the first that takes it (choose **None** to stop checking). Printers do not reach every edge of every label: the canvas shades what the printer does not print, and a dashed line marks how near a die-cut label's edge content may be cut off as labels shift. Elements reaching into either are outlined in amber and named. Warnings never stop printing. **Use its label size** takes the size from the printer's roll and scales the elements with it, and **Test print** prints one label as the template is on screen, saved or not, for the asset being previewed or with placeholders.

The template list shows which printers each template prints on, as their rolls are loaded now. Changing a printer's roll never changes a template: a template that does not suit the new roll waits until one it suits is loaded again. Before saving a new roll, the printer form says which labels waiting in the print queue would no longer fit, which categories' templates no printer would suit, and which pools' printers would no longer be loaded alike.

To gather labels over time and print them together, sorted onto several printers, use the [print queue](/help/spool).
