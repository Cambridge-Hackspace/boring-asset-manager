# Labels and printing

**Templates** lay out labels: text, codes such as QR codes, and images, at the size of your label stock. Text uses placeholders filled in for each asset, such as `{{asset.name}}`, `{{asset.location}}` or `{{field.serial}}`; the editor suggests them as you type.

`{{scan_url}}` is the address of an asset's scan page: a QR code of it takes whoever scans the label there. See [Scanning](/help/scanning).

The editor is laid out like a drawing program. The **toolbox** on the left holds the tools: **Select and move**, then **Text**, **Barcode or QR code** and **Image**, then **Rectangle**, **Ellipse**, **Line** and **Polygon**, and **Import SVG**. Choose one and drag on the label to draw what it adds, or click to place one at its usual size; chosen from the keyboard, it is added in the middle of the label. Across the top, the tool's options give the selection's position, size and angle in numbers. The panels on the right, **Properties**, **Layers**, **Arrange**, **Label**, **Printer** and **Preview data**, each fold away by their title and move up or down, and the editor remembers how you left them. The status bar along the bottom shows where the pointer is on the label and what you can do now.

Click an element to select it, and Shift+click to add or remove others. Drag to move, and drag a handle to resize; elements keep to the grid unless Alt is held, and Escape puts back a drag in progress. The arrow keys move the selection one grid step, ten with Shift, or a tenth with Alt. Ctrl+A selects everything, Ctrl+Shift+A or Escape nothing. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy and paste elements, between templates too, and pasting SVG imports it. Press Tab, with nothing focused, to hide everything but the label, and Tab again to bring it back.

To look closer, hold Ctrl and turn the mouse wheel, which zooms toward the pointer, or press + and -; the wheel alone scrolls. Hold Space, or the middle button, and drag to move around. 1 shows the label at 100%, 2 fits it, 3 fits its width, and 0 goes between fitting it and where you were. The zoom box at the bottom right takes a percentage. At 100%, each printer dot is one pixel of your screen; switch on **Print size** to see the label at its real size instead, and calibrate your screen once with a ruler, as browsers do not know how large screens are. The rulers mark where the pointer and the selection are; right-click one for millimeters or inches. The **Overview** panel shows the whole label: drag in it to move there.

Elements are drawn in order, each over those before it, so text can sit on an image. The **Layers** panel shows them with the top one first: drag a layer, or use the stacking buttons under **Arrange**, to change what is drawn over what. Where elements overlap, a click selects the top one. **Lock** an element in the layers so it is not picked up by accident. **Arrange** also lines the selection up, with each other or with the label, and spreads three or more out evenly; several selected can be grouped, so a click takes them all.

Any element can be turned, to any angle: type it in the tool options, or turn it a quarter turn left or right beside it. Several selected elements turn together, about the middle of them all. Codes turned a quarter turn print as sharply as upright ones; at other angles their edges follow the printer's dots, so check that a turned code scans with the scanners you use.

A template is **landscape** when wider than tall and **portrait** when taller. Switching it, or **Turn left** and **Turn right**, turns the label a quarter turn with everything on it, so the design reads the same, turned. **Use its label size** takes a printer's roll with its longer side across, and **Use it upright** the other way round. When a label fits the printer either way, as a square label or one from a continuous roll does, **Feed** chooses which way it goes through: as it fits, or with its width across or along the roll; the fit check says which. A template that fits several label sizes can also take labels turned a quarter turn, its content turned to match.

**Shapes** draw borders, dividers, boxes, arrows and backgrounds. Choose **Rectangle**, **Ellipse** or **Line** and drag on the label; a **Polygon** takes a point at each click, and is finished by a double click, Enter, or a click on its first point. Each shape has a stroke and a fill, in black, white or none: white paints over whatever is beneath it, so white text, or a white shape, shows on a black one. Text's **Color** can be white for that too. A rectangle's corners can be rounded. A stroke thinner than one printer dot prints one dot wide, and the editor says so.

**Import SVG** brings in a logo, symbol or icon drawn elsewhere, or drop the SVG file on the label: its shapes come in black or white, fitted into the middle of the label and selected together, and print sharp at any size. Colors become black or white by how light they look. Text is left out, as it needs the artwork's fonts, so convert it to paths before exporting the file; images, scripts and effects are left out too, and the editor says what was.

A code keeps a **margin** clear around it, which scanners use to find where it starts. Each side left empty has the code's standard quiet zone; a number sets it, counted in modules (the code's squares or bar widths, so the margin grows and shrinks with the code) or in millimeters or inches, the same whatever the code holds. **Across** and **Down** choose where the code sits in its box: centered, or against a side or corner. A margin narrower than the standard is allowed, since a code against the label's edge often scans, but the editor says so: try it with your scanners.

## Printing

On an asset's page, or for several assets ticked on the list, choose a template to download labels as SVG, PNG or PDF, or send them to a printer set up on the **Printers** page. **Images** holds logos and pictures for templates.

Its category's templates are listed first, and the first of them that can label it and that a printer takes is chosen. Only the printers a template fits can be chosen; the others are listed with why not, such as a roll of the wrong size loaded. A template that cannot label an asset, such as one printing a QR code for an asset that has none, says so in place of its preview. On an asset's page, **Add to the queue** keeps its label in the print queue, to print later with others.

Each printer has a roll loaded. Brother QL printers take Brother's own rolls, all of which are listed; TSPL printers such as the Rollo, and DYMO LabelWriters, also take rolls of any size, added under **Label stock** on the **Printers** page with their width, and for die-cut labels their length and the gap between them.

A template has **one label size**, printing only at its own size and DPI, or **fits several label sizes**: designed at one size, it is laid out again for each label it prints on, and at each printer's resolution, so one design serves 29 mm and 62 mm tape, or a Brother and a Rollo. Choose under **Sizing** in the editor, with the narrowest, widest, shortest and tallest labels it takes. Each element then says how it follows the label across and down: in proportion, kept to one side (left or right, top or bottom), stretched between both, or kept in the middle. It can also have a smallest and largest size, and say what happens where it does not fit: the label is refused, it is left off, or something else takes its place, such as the code as text. The editor shows the template on each printer as loaded, and at any size you type, with the sizes it fits in its shape given the longest codes assigned now.

The template editor draws the label as it will print, dot for dot, as you drag, turn and type. Opening the editor loads its renderer first, which takes a moment; until then, or if your browser cannot run it, the label is drawn by the server shortly after each change instead.

A template prints on a printer when its size and DPI suit the roll loaded. The template editor checks against a printer as you design: the one you last chose for the template, or else the first that takes it (choose **None** to stop checking). Printers do not reach every edge of every label: the canvas shades what the printer does not print, and a dashed line marks how near a die-cut label's edge content may be cut off as labels shift. Elements reaching into either are outlined in amber and named. Warnings never stop printing. **Use its label size** takes the size from the printer's roll and scales the elements with it, and **Test print** prints one label as the template is on screen, saved or not, for the asset being previewed or with placeholders.

The template list shows which printers each template prints on, as their rolls are loaded now. Changing a printer's roll never changes a template: a template that does not suit the new roll waits until one it suits is loaded again. Before saving a new roll, the printer form says which labels waiting in the print queue would no longer fit, which categories' templates no printer would suit, and which pools' printers would no longer be loaded alike.

To gather labels over time and print them together, sorted onto several printers, use the [print queue](/help/spool).
