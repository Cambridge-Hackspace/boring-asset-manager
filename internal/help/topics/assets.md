# Assets

An asset is anything you keep track of. Open **Assets** to list them; search by name, code, or where it is (searching "shelf a" finds what is on Shelf A), or filter by category, condition, loan or favorites. The **Where** column says what each is in.

## Favorites

Star an asset you use often, beside its name on its page or in the list, to find it on the home page under **Favorites**, and with **Only favorites** in the list. Categories, reports and flows have a star too. Your favorites are yours alone; one you can no longer see is hidden until you can again.

## Adding and editing

Choose **New asset**, give it a name and its categories, say where it is (find the shelf, box or room it is in), and fill in the fields its categories ask for. Fields marked as required must be filled in. Codes for its labels are under **Codes**: type one, choose **Assign the next code** to take the one after the highest of that kind (after HS-105, HS-106), or leave them for later.

**Duplicate…**, under **More** on an asset's page, starts a new asset like it, such as one more spool of the same filament: the form is filled in with its categories, fields, stock and place, and the next code in its series (after SP-014, SP-015). Its links are listed to keep or untick, though not what it contains, which stays where it is, and so are its photos, copied unless unticked. Change what differs, and nothing is saved until you choose **Create asset**. Its condition and history are its own.

To add several alike, such as a box of new spools, **Create and add another** creates one and keeps the form for the next, with the next code in its series, so each one after the first is a name and a code.

You can also start a new asset from where it goes: **Add something inside…** under **More** on a shelf or a bin's page, **Add an asset to it** on a category's page, or **New asset…** in an asset's **Links**, which links the new one as the link chosen there would.

**Edit** changes any of this; every change is kept in the asset's **History**, with who made it.

## Purchase

An asset's **Purchase** section says where it came from: the **vendor** it was bought from, when it was **ordered** and **received**, its **price** and currency, the **order or invoice number**, when its **warranty ends**, and its **invoice**, one of its attachments. Fill them in under **Purchase** when you add or edit it; every one is optional, and the invoice can be chosen once the file is attached. Duplicating an asset leaves them out: the new one was bought on its own.

Seeing the price, order number and invoice takes **See prices**, which roles that read assets have unless it is taken away; without it, they are hidden, and editing the asset leaves them as they are. Before a warranty ends, a notification channel can tell you (see [Notifications](/help/notifications)).

## Contracts

The **Contracts** section lists the contracts covering the asset, such as *Under maintenance contract with Acme until June 2027*: those naming it, and those naming one of its categories, each linking to the contract. Its own warranty end, above, is the warranty it came with; an extended warranty is a contract (see [Contracts](/help/contracts)).

## Condition

Anyone allowed can **Report a problem**: that it needs maintenance, or is out of service, with a note saying what is wrong. Someone who edits assets marks it working again when it is fixed. Assets out of service cannot be lent.

## Attachments and links

Attach files under **Attachments** on the asset's page: photos (JPEG or PNG), documents (PDFs, plain text, Markdown and CSV files), and any other types your instance allows. Rename or describe each with **Edit**, and put them in order with the arrows; the first photo is shown in lists. Photos show on the page; documents download when you open them, so nothing uploaded can run in your browser. Duplicating an asset copies its attachments too, unless you untick them. **Links** join assets to each other: a bin *contains* screws, a drill is *used with* its bits. Where something is follows its links: a bin on a shelf, screws in the bin. Its page shows **Where** it is, with **Move** to put it somewhere else, and an asset that holds others lists them under **Contents**.

## Archiving

**Archive** an asset you no longer have. It leaves the lists and its codes become free, but its history stays, and it can be restored.
